Understanding Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the growth those conversion-tax dollars could have earned in a taxable account. In the do-nothing baseline they stay invested, so they are credited to the no-conversion side. Capital gains tax is applied only to growth above the original tax payments.

Key Insight: The conversion wins when its total after-tax wealth — tax-free Roth plus any remaining IRA after-tax — exceeds the do-nothing wealth: the full IRA taxed at the effective retirement rate, plus reinvested RMDs and the tax dollars left invested.

Tax Basis
Projections use 2026 federal income tax brackets and standard deductions for the selected filing status (married filing jointly: $32,200; single: $16,100), reflecting the IRS 2026 inflation adjustments (Rev. Proc. 2025-32) as amended by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA). OBBBA made the current 10/12/22/24/32/35/37% rate schedule permanent, so no reversion to pre-2018 rates is assumed. Where the modeled age reaches 65 during tax years 2025–2028, the analysis applies the temporary OBBBA senior bonus deduction ($6,000 single / $12,000 MFJ, phasing out above $75,000 / $150,000 of MAGI). Social Security is taxed under the provisional-income method (0/50/85%); when enabled, Medicare IRMAA Part B/D surcharges (age 63+) are estimated from representative tiers. Required minimum distributions begin at age 73 (SECURE 2.0). State tax schedules (NY, NJ, CA) use their standard schedule and are illustrative; confirm all figures against current published rates for the applicable tax year.
